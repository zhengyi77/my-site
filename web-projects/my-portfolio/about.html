My story

I study computer science at Hainan Bielefeld University of Applied Sciences, where half of my courses are taught in German and half in English. That mix is probably why I care about writing things clearly — a sentence that only works in one language is a sentence that is not finished.

I came to the web sideways. I started by wanting a page of my own, then discovered that a page is really a set of promises: that a link leads somewhere, that an image has a size before it loads, that a form tells you what went wrong. Most of what I do now is keeping those promises.

In this course I built a six-page learning website from scratch — a quotes page, a project list, a useless machine — and then this portfolio as my personal project. Both are in separate repositories with their own history, because that is how the assignment is meant to work: two things that share nothing except the person who made them.

What I want next is straightforward: to get faster without getting sloppier, and to build something that other people actually use.
